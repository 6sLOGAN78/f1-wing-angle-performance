# Assumptions and limitations register

This study is a representative educational model, not a reconstruction of any team's confidential Formula 1 car. The numbers are suitable for controlled comparisons inside the stated model and are not predictions of a named car, driver, tyre compound, or circuit.

## Model-scope assumptions

- The car is treated as a point mass following a prescribed closed path; yaw dynamics, steering transients, suspension kinematics, heave, pitch, roll, kerbs, traffic, wind, and driver adaptation are outside scope.
- Rear-wing angle is the controlled variable. Front-wing, floor, diffuser, cooling, wheel-wake, and body coefficients are collected into fixed baseline coefficients.
- Air density is constant within each run. Crosswind and gusts are omitted.
- The wing uses a finite-wing lift slope, parabolic induced drag, and a smooth empirical post-stall decay. It is not a CFD- or wind-tunnel-derived multi-element map.
- Aero forces vary with dynamic pressure and the declared coefficient model. Ride-height and yaw multipliers exist in the code path but nominal results use zero yaw and nominal ride height.
- The tyre model uses load-sensitive friction coefficients and a friction ellipse. It omits temperature, pressure, wear, relaxation length, camber, combined-slip asymmetry, and detailed transient behaviour.
- Wheel force is obtained from a representative torque curve, fixed ratios, driveline efficiency, rear-wheel drive, and a redline-limited gear choice. Energy recovery and hybrid deployment are not modeled.
- Braking force is constrained by the tyre friction envelope and a global maximum; detailed brake balance, thermal limits, migration, and control-system logic are omitted.
- Synthetic track archetypes are used to isolate circuit-character effects. They are not surveyed reproductions of actual Formula 1 venues.
- The quasi-steady solver assumes locally equilibrated performance and applies forward/backward spatial passes. It does not integrate a full multibody transient model.

## Active-aero assumptions

- Open and closed wing targets are idealized angle commands within 0–30 degrees.
- Ideal-active results are an upper-bound comparison without actuator delay or slew limits.
- Actuator-limited results include specified activation/deactivation delays and a 90 degree/s rate limit, but omit motor current, compliance, backlash, sensor filtering, failure modes, and regulatory state logic.
- The schedule optimizer is deliberately low dimensional. A slower actuator-limited result than the optimum fixed wing is therefore a valid model outcome, not an error; it indicates that actuation and switching penalties can erase the ideal aerodynamic advantage.

## Evidence and validation limitations

- No proprietary geometry, telemetry, CFD field, wind-tunnel data, tyre rig data, or track measurement was available.
- MATLAB files are statically checked and compared against Python-exported reference fixtures, but are not runtime-tested in this authoring environment. MATLAB R2022b+ execution is required for final independent parity confirmation.
- The Simulink `.slx` file is generated by `build_simulink_demo.m` when a licensed Simulink installation is available; it is a focused longitudinal/aerodynamic demonstration rather than the full spatial lap solver.
- The Monte Carlo study uses bounded educational distributions and a first-order response surface anchored to full-solver cases. It characterizes model sensitivity, not real-world probability.
- Numerical repeatability is checked through deterministic inputs, a fixed random seed, input/output hashes, contract tests, and cross-format comparisons. Physical validation remains future work.
